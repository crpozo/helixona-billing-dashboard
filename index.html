How to read these numbers

Short definitions of every figure on this page.

BilledTotal charges sent out at the clinic's full fee schedule, before any insurer discount.
Example: a Mito Boost infusion billed to Blue Shield at $1,650.
CollectedMoney that actually came in on those claims, from insurers and patients.
Blue Shield paid $420 and the patient a $50 copay: Collected = $470.
Contractual adjustmentsThe insurer's contract discount: the part of the fee that was never going to be paid because the contracted rate is lower. Not a loss, it is the real price of the service with that insurer.
Blue Shield's allowed amount is $900: Contractual = $750.
Write-offsMoney that was collectible and the clinic chose to give up: denials not appealed, late filing, small balances, courtesy discounts. Real money lost.
$200 denied for missing authorization and never appealed: Write-off = $200.
Balance still dueWhat is still open and someone owes, insurer or patient. Billed always equals Collected + Contractual + Write-offs + Balance due.
$1,650 − 470 − 750 − 200 = $230 still due.
Collection rateCollected ÷ (Billed − Contractual). Of what was realistic to collect, how much came in. The contract discount is excluded because that money never existed.
$470 ÷ ($1,650 − $750) = 52%.
Net change in A/RBilled − Collected − Contractual − Write-offs. How much the receivable grew over the period, net of credits.
Open balance by ageBalance still due, grouped by how long ago the service happened. The older the bucket, the harder it is to collect.
Outstanding (A/R)The official total of every unpaid balance in eCW on the report date, aged by claim date. The number to quote as "what we are owed".
How to read a payer rowHigh contractual with a low balance: the payer adjudicates and pays. Low contractual with a high balance: the payer has not responded. High write-offs: money was let go, ask why.
Claim status codeThe workflow state eCW assigned to the claim (submitted, paid, denied, patient balance, etc.). Meaning of each code to be confirmed with billing.
